Using Fotobogen

Everything is done in a web browser. Your site is at its address; the admin is at the same address followed by /admin.

The admin

Log in at /admin with the account you made during setup. You arrive at Overview, which shows the site and its server at a glance. Every other page is in the menu down the left side (behind a Menu button on a phone):

A login lasts 30 days from when you last used it. Five wrong passwords in 15 minutes from one address locks that address out for the rest of the 15 minutes.

Uploading

Choose Upload, then pick files or drop them on the page. Each file shows its progress and what became of it.

  • Formats: JPEG, PNG, WebP, TIFF, and AVIF. A file is judged by what is inside it, not by its name.
  • Size: up to 50 MB and 100 megapixels each. AVIF is held to 16 megapixels, WebP to 60, and interlaced PNG to 40, because those need far more memory to open. A larger one is refused with a note to upload a JPEG instead.
  • Duplicates: the same file is not stored twice. The page names the photo that already has it.
  • Your file is kept untouched. Fotobogen makes resized copies for the web (up to 2560 pixels on the long side) and never enlarges anything.
  • Processing happens one photo at a time in the background, so you can keep uploading. A photo that cannot be processed after three tries is marked failed, with the reason and a Try again button, here and on the photo's own page in the library.

Once processed, a photo is on your site, unless you have set new uploads to start as drafts.

The library

Library shows every photograph. The first one there is the first on your site.

  • Order: drag a photo onto another to put it in front, or use its Up, Down, and To top buttons.
  • Several at once: tick photos, then Hide, Show, Delete, add to an album, or add a tag. Deleting asks first and removes the uploaded file and every copy of it.
  • Find: show only hidden photos, photos with no alt text, or photos that failed.

Editing a photo

Open a photo from the library to change:

  • its title and description;
  • its alt text, which describes the picture to people who cannot see it. Screen readers read it aloud, and it is worth writing for every photo;
  • its web address (the last part of /photo/…) and the date taken;
  • its albums and tags;
  • when it is published, its licence, and whether it can be downloaded;
  • its look: a strip of small previews under the photo shows what each switched-on module would do. Choosing one shows a larger preview, and nothing changes until you save.

Beside the form is what the camera recorded in the file: camera, lens, exposure, and place.

Links to a photo

Further down the same page is a Links panel: the full address of the photo's page, the address of each size of the picture, and a line of HTML that shows the photo linked to its page. Each has a Copy button. Use them in an email, a post, or another website.

The pictures are public files: anyone with a picture's address can open it, with no login, and any website can show it. Nothing needs setting up for that (no CORS configuration); Fotobogen tells browsers that other sites may read its photos. Nothing checks who asks for a picture, either: its address has a long name that cannot be guessed, and keeps working for anyone who has it, even after the photo is hidden, until the photo is deleted or rendered again. The links begin with your site's address. On your own machine they begin with http://localhost:5150, which works only there, until you set the address under Admin, Search and sharing or as SITE_URL on the server.

Deleting, and the trash

Deleting a photo, from the library or from its own page, moves it to the trash. It is off your site at once: the grid, its page, its albums, the feed. It is kept for 30 days, with the file you uploaded, and then removed for good.

In the library, choose Show, Trash to see what is there and the day each photo will go. Tick photos and press:

  • Restore, to put them back as they were: the same address, albums, tags, and place in the order. A restored photo is made again from the file you uploaded, so it takes a moment to reappear.
  • Delete for good, to remove them now. This asks first, and cannot be undone: the uploaded file and every resized copy are deleted.

Two things to know. Uploading a file that is in the trash is refused, with a note saying where it is: restore it, or delete it for good first. And while a photo is in the trash its picture files still exist, so someone who already has a picture's own address can still open it; to withdraw those addresses, delete the photo for good.

Publishing, scheduling, and drafts

On a photo's page, Publish has three choices:

  • Public: shown on your site now.
  • Scheduled: public from a date and time you pick, in your own time zone. Until then the photo is in your library but not on the site, the feed, or the sitemap. It appears by itself when the time comes.
  • Hidden: a draft only you see.

In Settings you can make every new upload start as a draft, so nothing is public until you say so.

Albums and tags

  • Albums are named sets you arrange yourself. Create them under Admin, Albums; add photos from the library (tick photos, choose an album, Add to album) or from a photo's page. A photo can be in several. Each public album has a page at /album/…, and they are listed at /albums. An album can be hidden. Deleting an album never deletes its photos.
  • Tags are short labels typed on a photo's page, separated by commas, or added to several photos at once from the library. Each has a page at /tag/…. A tag exists only while a photo carries it.

Private albums

An album can be private: for a client, a family, a few friends. Under Admin, Albums, edit the album and choose Private. It gets a link that cannot be guessed, shown in the album's entry with a Copy button. Send that link to the people who may see the album.

  • Whoever has the link sees the album, and each photo in it, with no login. Nobody else does: it is not on the albums page, in the menu, in search, in the feed, or in the sitemap.
  • It can hold hidden photos, which stay off your site. The usual way: upload, hide the photos, add them to the album, make it private, send the link. To have every upload start hidden, switch that on in Settings.
  • Make a new link replaces the link at once, and the old one stops working for everyone who has it. Making the album public or hidden ends the link too.
  • A link is only as private as the people who hold it: anyone can pass it on. Fotobogen tells search engines to keep away, never lets a browser or proxy store the pages, does not send the link on to other sites, and puts no counting script on these pages.
  • A private album has no slideshow and offers no downloads, and its photos show no tags, other albums, or location.

What visitors see

  • The grid, at your address: photos newest first, thirty to a page, each keeping its shape or all cut square. It works with JavaScript switched off, and nothing jumps as images arrive.
  • A photo: the picture, its title, description, date, camera details, and size in pixels, with previous and next. It opens over the grid or on a page of its own, as you chose. A Full screen button appears over the photo when you point at it, and a click on the photo does the same; Escape leaves it. Photos with no camera data, such as the samples, show only their size.
  • Albums and tags, each with its own page.
  • The slideshow, at /slideshow: full screen, one photo after another, for the whole site or one album or tag. Pressing a photo shows its details. Space pauses, the arrow keys step, F is full screen, Escape leaves. Seconds per photo and the order are in Settings.
  • A light and dark switch at the top. More about that.
  • An RSS feed at /feed.xml, a sitemap for search engines, and a preview card when a photo's address is shared.

Only photos that are processed and public are ever shown. Where a photo was taken is never shown unless you switch that on.

Settings

Admin, Settings holds:

  • the site title and tagline;
  • the grid (photos keeping their shape, or squares) and whether a photo opens over the grid or on its own page;
  • privacy: show camera details (on to begin with) and show where photos were taken (off to begin with);
  • the licence your photos are published under: all rights reserved, or one of the Creative Commons licences. It is shown on every photo page, and a photo can have its own;
  • downloads: whether visitors may download photos (off to begin with). A download is a large copy with all camera data removed. The file exactly as you uploaded it is handed out only by a site that also shows where photos were taken, because that file carries everything the camera recorded;
  • new uploads start as drafts;
  • picture quality: Smaller files, Standard, or High. This is how much the copies shown on your site are compressed. Changing it makes every photo's copies again in the background;
  • the slideshow's timing and order.

Making the site your own

Nothing a visitor sees has to say "Fotobogen". It is your site, at your address, under your name.

Site text

Admin, Site text holds the site's title and tagline, and every fixed piece of text on the public pages: the links at the top, the buttons, the labels beside a photo, the slideshow's controls, and what is shown when a page cannot be found. Each field shows the wording used now. Type your own to replace it; leave it empty to keep the default.

  • There is a line of your own for the footer, empty to begin with: a copyright notice, for instance.
  • Language is a code such as en, de, or pt-BR. It tells screen readers how to pronounce your site and search engines what language it is in. It does not translate anything; you do that by rewording the fields.
  • One button puts every field back to its default.

Admin, Search and sharing decides what search engines show for your site and what appears when someone shares a link to it. Every field is optional, and the page previews the result as you type.

How it looks

Skins change the look, the colours, and light or dark.

Sample photos

Fotobogen can download 25 photographs so that a new site has something to show. They come from Lorem Picsum (Unsplash photographs, credited to their photographers) and are not part of the software. Overview has Download sample photos (Download the rest when some are already there), and Remove sample photos deletes all of them, and only them.

Backups and updates

Admin, System shows the version, what is stored, and the free space, and has Download a backup, restore, and Check for updates. When a newer release is found, a note on every admin page says so, and the System page can install it for you. Take a backup regularly and keep it somewhere other than the server. Backing up and restoring are covered with the questions.

Uploading from a script or another program

Under API tokens, make a token of the Upload only kind and copy it. A program then sends it with each request, in place of logging in. From a terminal:

curl -H "Authorization: Bearer fb_YOUR_TOKEN" \
     -F "file=@photo.jpg" \
     https://photos.example.com/api/admin/photos

The answer says whether the photo was accepted, and its id. To upload a whole folder:

for photo in *.jpg; do
  curl -sS -H "Authorization: Bearer fb_YOUR_TOKEN" -F "file=@$photo" \
       https://photos.example.com/api/admin/photos
  echo
done
  • The same limits apply as in the browser: format, size, and no duplicates.
  • Use HTTPS. Over plain HTTP the token can be read on the way.
  • Treat a token like a password, give each program its own, and revoke one that is no longer needed. The page shows when each was last used.
  • A token is for the API only. It does not open the admin's pages, and it cannot change settings or reach your backups.

Keyboard and accessibility

The admin and the public site are built to WCAG 2.1 level AA and are tested for it on every change: everything can be reached and worked from the keyboard, text has a contrast of at least 4.5 to 1 in light and dark, and nothing scrolls sideways on a phone. Motion is reduced when your device asks for that. If you find something that does not meet this, it is a bug: please report it.
