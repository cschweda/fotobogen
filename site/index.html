Fotobogen is software for running your own photo website. Install it once, sign in to the dashboard, and upload. Your pictures appear on a clean public site at your own web address, each with its title, description, and camera details.

If you have ever set up a blog on your own hosting, it will feel familiar: an installer, a dashboard, themes, and add-ons. This one is built for photographs instead of posts. There is no feed, no algorithm, and no subscription.

The name is German for “photo sheet,” as in a contact sheet.

  1. 01

    Install

    Set it up on a small, low-cost web server. Answer a few questions and create your login.

  2. 02

    Upload

    Add photos from the dashboard. Give each one a title and a description.

  3. 03

    Publish

    Visitors see a grid of your work. Each photo opens large, with its details beside it.

  4. 04

    Make it yours

    Change the look with a skin. Add features such as black-and-white conversion with modules.

A look around

These are real screens from the current build. The installer is a web page: you open your new site in a browser and answer six short steps. Then you upload, and your site is live.

  1. 01

    Prove the server is yours

    When Fotobogen first starts it prints a one-time token. The setup page asks for it, so only the person running the server can claim the site.

    Setup, step 1 of 6

  2. 02

    Create your login

    One account, one password, with a meter that says how strong it is. There is no email and nothing to sign up for.

    Setup, step 3 of 6

  3. 03

    Choose how photos are shown

    Pick a grid and a way to open photos from small diagrams. Both can be changed later.

    Setup, step 4 of 6

  4. 04

    Start empty, or with samples

    The installer can fetch 25 placeholder photographs so the site has something to show. A counter follows the download, which never holds you up and can be cancelled or skipped.

    Setup, step 6 of 6

  5. 05

    Sign in to the dashboard

    The dashboard sits behind your login. From here you upload, and download or remove the sample photos whenever you like.

    Dashboard

  6. 06

    Upload

    Choose files or drop them on the page. Each photo is checked, stored untouched, and resized for every screen; anything that is not a photo, or is already in your library, is explained on its own row.

    Upload

  7. 07

    Your photographs, published

    Visitors see a grid that keeps each photo its own shape, or equal squares if you prefer. It is laid out without scripts, loads a blurred placeholder first, and never jumps as pictures arrive.

    The public site

  8. 08

    One photo, with its words

    Each photo has its own page and address, with its title, description, date, and camera details, and links to the next and the previous. It can also open over the grid.

    A photo page

  9. 09

    Film looks and frames

    Modules change how a photo looks without touching the file you uploaded. Under each photo is a strip of previews: black-and-white, a colour slide, a contact-print frame with made-up film markings. Pick one, adjust it, save. Switching it off puts the photo back.

    Editing a photo

  10. 10

    A library you can arrange

    Drag photos into order or use the keyboard. Hide, show, tag, or delete several at once. Group them into albums. Schedule one to appear later.

    The library

The photographs in these screens are the sample photos Fotobogen can download for you: Unsplash photographs, served by Lorem Picsum. The pictures in the upload screen are generated gradients.

Where it runs

Not here. This page is only an introduction. Fotobogen runs on a server you control, and your photographs stay on it.

  1. Your serverFotobogen and all your photographs, in one folder you can back up or move.
  3. Your addressA domain name you own, pointing at that server.
  5. Any browserVisitors see the gallery. You sign in from anywhere to manage it.

There is no hosted version, no account with us, and nothing to install on the computers you browse from.

  • ServerA small Linux server is enough: 1 GB of memory, about $6 a month. A Mac or Windows PC works for trying it out.
  • SoftwareDocker, or Node 24. Nothing else.
  • Outside servicesNone. No database server, no cloud storage, no API keys.
